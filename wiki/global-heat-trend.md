# The global heat trend behind the local numbers

## Deaths

The 2025 Lancet Countdown (128 experts, 71 institutions) reports that heat is "killing nearly 550,000 people every year" worldwide — "a 63 per cent jump since the 1990s" ([Lancet Countdown 2025](../sources/lancet-countdown-2025-heat-deaths.md)).

## Who is most exposed

- **Adults over 65 face a threefold rise** in heatwave days compared with the 1990s.
- **Babies under one** now face "nearly four times as many heatwave days" as in the 1990s.

([Lancet Countdown 2025](../sources/lancet-countdown-2025-heat-deaths.md))

## How much is climate change

**84%** of the heatwave days people experienced between 2020 and 2024 "would not have occurred without human-induced climate change" ([Lancet Countdown 2025](../sources/lancet-countdown-2025-heat-deaths.md)).

## The policy backdrop

Global temperatures in 2025 were "1.43°C above pre-industrial levels," and CO₂ concentrations reached "152% above pre-industrial levels" in 2024 ([UN SDG 13 targets](../sources/un-sdg13-targets.md)). Disaster-related mortality (all disaster types, not heat alone) fell 65% over the past decade, and 141 countries had adopted national disaster risk reduction strategies by 2025 (same source).

## ⚠️ This is a global figure, not a stand-in for LA or Korea

The Lancet Countdown's ~550,000/year is a worldwide estimate covering 2012–2021 with its own methodology. It should not be added to, compared directly against, or used to sanity-check the LA County (2015–2024, county-recorded) or Korea KDCA (since 2011, national) death counts — see the flag in [the Lancet Countdown source](../sources/lancet-countdown-2025-heat-deaths.md) itself.
