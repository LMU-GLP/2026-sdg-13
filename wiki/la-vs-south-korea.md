# Los Angeles vs. South Korea: comparing before choosing a region

A side-by-side of what our sources actually say about each region. The region isn't picked yet — this page is for that decision.

## Who is dying, and where

| | Los Angeles County | South Korea |
|---|---|---|
| Recorded heat deaths | **125** across 2015–2024, officially recorded ([LA extreme heat brief](../sources/la-extreme-heat.md)) | **238** since monitoring began in 2011 ([Korea heat deaths, elderly](../sources/korea-heat-deaths-elderly.md)) |
| Who's most affected | Older adults named among highest-risk groups; ER visits average 898/year, peaking at 1,352 (2018) ([LA extreme heat brief](../sources/la-extreme-heat.md)) | **156 of 238 deaths (over 65%) were aged 60+**; 4 of 7 deaths in 2025 (through July 8) were aged 70+ ([Korea heat deaths, elderly](../sources/korea-heat-deaths-elderly.md)) |
| Where it happens | Not broken out by location in our source | **32% on farms, 15% at home, ~14% near roads** ([Korea heat deaths, elderly](../sources/korea-heat-deaths-elderly.md)) |
| The known undercount | County's own Medical Examiner study estimates up to **1,265 additional deaths** (2014–2019) beyond the official 125 ([LA extreme heat brief](../sources/la-extreme-heat.md)) | No equivalent undercount estimate appears in our source |

### ⚠️ Do not compare the 125 and 238 directly

As the Korea source itself warns: Korea counts nationwide heat-illness deaths since 2011; LA counts one county's officially recorded heat deaths across a different decade, and LA's own health department says that number is probably too low. These are not the same measurement at the same scale, and adding or ranking them against each other would be misleading ([Korea heat deaths, elderly](../sources/korea-heat-deaths-elderly.md); [LA extreme heat brief](../sources/la-extreme-heat.md)).

## The shape of the problem

LA's problem is **urban and chronic**: a heat season that's grown 46 days longer since the 1960s, hitting a broad population with ER visits and hospitalizations tracked year over year across a dense county ([LA extreme heat brief](../sources/la-extreme-heat.md)).

Korea's problem, per our source, is **rural and acute**: nearly two-thirds of deaths are elderly people, close to a third of them on farms, often alone — illustrated by two named cases of women in their 80s who died doing agricultural work by themselves ([Korea heat deaths, elderly](../sources/korea-heat-deaths-elderly.md)).

## What's already being done

LA has a chief heat officer, 22 augmented cooling centers (up from 6), a public cooling-options app, and a county Heat Ambassador program ([LA Chief Heat Officer program](../sources/la-chief-heat-officer-program.md); [LA extreme heat brief](../sources/la-extreme-heat.md)). Korea, in our sources, has only a work-hours advisory for outdoor laborers ([Korea heat deaths, elderly](../sources/korea-heat-deaths-elderly.md)) — see [existing programs](heat-safety-programs.md) for the fuller picture. That gap may itself be worth investigating rather than assumed to mean nothing exists.

## What this means for choosing a region

- **LA** has richer, multi-year public health data (ER visits, hospitalizations, a Medical Examiner risk study) and an existing, fairly developed institutional response to build on or critique.
- **Korea**, per our sources, has a narrower but sharper demographic finding (elderly farmworkers, often alone) and, so far, less documented institutional response to critique or extend.

Neither region is disqualified by the evidence gathered so far; the choice depends on which problem — the diffuse, if it is a big city, versus concentrated, rural — the team wants to design for.
