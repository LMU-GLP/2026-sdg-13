# Los Angeles: extreme heat and the county's response

## The trend

LA County's Department of Public Health counted **509 extreme heat days** (90°F+ in downtown LA) between 2005 and 2024, and says the heat season itself "is 46 days longer than it was in the 1960s," measured from the first heat wave to the last ([LA extreme heat brief](../sources/la-extreme-heat.md)).

## Health impact

- ER visits for heat-related illness (2005–2022) ranged from a low of **456** (2005) to a high of **1,352** (2018), averaging **898 a year**; **1,195** in 2022 alone.
- Hospital admissions over the same period ranged from **96** (2011) to **461** (2018), averaging **196 a year**.
- July produces the **most ER visits** (4,020 across 2005–2022) even though August and September have more extreme heat days — the brief attributes this to early-season heat hitting unacclimatised bodies hardest.
- A 2024 Medical Examiner study found deaths rose **7%, 15%, and 66%** on days of moderate, major, and extreme HeatRisk, compared with no-risk days.

(All figures: [LA extreme heat brief](../sources/la-extreme-heat.md))

## The undercount, in the county's own words

The county officially recorded **125 heat deaths across 2015–2024** — but its own Medical Examiner analysis estimates heat "may have led to as many as 1,265 additional deaths between 2014-2019." Those two figures describe the same problem and differ by an order of magnitude, because heat usually kills indirectly (a death certificate says "heart failure," not "heat"). See [LA extreme heat brief](../sources/la-extreme-heat.md) for both numbers side by side.

Older adults are named among the county's highest-risk groups ([LA extreme heat brief](../sources/la-extreme-heat.md)).

## What the county already does

- Heat alerts by text and email subscription
- A schools toolkit
- A **Heat Ambassador** community-health-worker initiative, funded by the LARC-HEAT grant
- A County Heat Action Plan

([LA extreme heat brief](../sources/la-extreme-heat.md))

## The Chief Heat Officer program

Los Angeles appointed **Marta Segura** as its first chief heat officer in **June 2022**; she also leads the city's Climate Emergency Mobilization Office. Under this program, augmented cooling centers in underserved neighborhoods expanded **from 6 to 22 locations**, and the city built the **Cool Spots LA** app, which maps **more than 200 cooling options** (libraries, recreation centers, pools, hydration stations, shade structures) using GIS equity mapping.

During the **August 2022 heat dome**, LA emergency departments treated **an additional 1,500 patients daily**, and the city says it "now experiences six times more heatwaves than historically."

> "Some neighborhoods without trees, shade, and open space suffer four times the number of hospitalizations and premature deaths than areas with a greener, more climate-adapted infrastructure."

([LA Chief Heat Officer program](../sources/la-chief-heat-officer-program.md))
