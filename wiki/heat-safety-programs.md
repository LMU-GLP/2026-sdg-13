# Existing programs: what's already being tried

## Los Angeles

- **Chief Heat Officer** — Marta Segura, appointed June 2022, also leads the city's Climate Emergency Mobilization Office. Cooling centers in underserved neighborhoods expanded **from 6 to 22**, and the **Cool Spots LA** app maps **200+ cooling options** using GIS equity mapping ([LA Chief Heat Officer program](../sources/la-chief-heat-officer-program.md)).
- **County-level measures**: heat alerts by text/email, a schools toolkit, a **Heat Ambassador** community-health-worker initiative (LARC-HEAT grant funded), and a County Heat Action Plan ([LA extreme heat brief](../sources/la-extreme-heat.md)).

## South Korea

Only one response is documented in our sources: a government advisory recommending agricultural workers "work in early mornings or late evenings and avoid going outdoors in the hours between noon and 5 p.m." ([Korea heat deaths, elderly](../sources/korea-heat-deaths-elderly.md)). No cooling-center network, officer role, or app appears in any source collected so far — a real gap in our evidence, not a claim that Korea has no such program.

## Why this matters for a new proposal

Any new idea for **Los Angeles** has to sit alongside an already-fairly-developed response (chief heat officer, 22 cooling centers, a mapping app, community ambassadors) — the open question is who it's still failing to reach, since older adults remain a named highest-risk group despite these programs.

Any new idea for **South Korea** is working with far less documented existing infrastructure in our sources, but a differently shaped problem: elderly agricultural workers, often alone, on farms rather than in a dense city — which a cooling-center model (built for a dense LA-style city) may not fit.
