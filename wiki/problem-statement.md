# Problem statement

## Region

**South Korea.**

## Who, specifically

Elderly people (aged 60 and older) doing outdoor agricultural work, often alone.

## How many

- Since South Korea's Korea Disease Control and Prevention Agency (KDCA) began monitoring in 2011, it has recorded **238 heat-related deaths** total. Of those, **156 — over 65% — were aged 60 or older** ([Korea heat deaths, elderly](../sources/korea-heat-deaths-elderly.md)).
- Separately, of the same 238 deaths, **32% happened on farms** (15% at home, roughly 14% near roads) ([Korea heat deaths, elderly](../sources/korea-heat-deaths-elderly.md)).
- **These two figures are not cross-tabulated in our source.** We can say 65%+ of deaths were elderly, and separately that 32% were on farms — but we do not have a citable number for how many of the farm deaths were specifically elderly people. That overlap is a gap in our evidence, not a number we're supplying.
- In **2025, as of July 8, seven deaths** had been recorded, **four of them aged 70 or older** ([Korea heat deaths, elderly](../sources/korea-heat-deaths-elderly.md)).

## What our sources say about them

- Two named cases illustrate the shape of the problem: a woman in her 80s found unconscious near a greenhouse in Jinju, and another woman in her 80s found dead in a rice paddy on Jeju Island — both elderly women doing agricultural work alone ([Korea heat deaths, elderly](../sources/korea-heat-deaths-elderly.md)).
- The government's current advice is for agricultural workers to **"work in early mornings or late evenings and avoid going outdoors in the hours between noon and 5 p.m."** ([Korea heat deaths, elderly](../sources/korea-heat-deaths-elderly.md)) — advice that assumes a worker has someone else available to cover midday work, which a solo elderly farmer may not.
- No source we've collected documents a dedicated cooling-center network, check-in system, or outreach program for this population in South Korea — only this one work-hours advisory ([Korea heat deaths, elderly](../sources/korea-heat-deaths-elderly.md); [Existing heat-safety programs](heat-safety-programs.md)). That absence is a gap in our evidence, not proof that nothing exists.

## The statement

In South Korea, elderly people doing outdoor agricultural work are dying from extreme heat at a disproportionate rate. Since national monitoring began in 2011, over 65% of the country's 238 recorded heat deaths have been people aged 60 or older, and close to a third of all heat deaths happen on farms — work that can mean being alone for hours, as in the two specific cases in our source, both women in their 80s who died working unaccompanied. The government's documented response is a single piece of advice — avoid outdoor work between noon and 5 p.m. — with no cooling center, check-in system, or outreach program for this group appearing anywhere in our sources. This is who and where our project should focus: elderly agricultural workers, often working alone, in the hours the government itself has already identified as the most dangerous.
