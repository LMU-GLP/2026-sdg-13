The problem

Since South Korea's Korea Disease Control and Prevention Agency (KDCA) began monitoring in 2011, it has recorded 238 heat-related deaths. 156 of them — over 65% — were aged 60 or older, and close to a third happened on farms. (Korea heat deaths, elderly)

The government's own advice is for agricultural workers to

"work in early mornings or late evenings and avoid going outdoors in the hours between noon and 5 p.m."
(Korea heat deaths, elderly) — advice that assumes someone else can cover the midday work. For a farmer working alone, there may be no one else.

Full write-up: wiki/problem-statement.md

The concept: a heat check-in service

Who it's for

The elderly farmer is enrolled and uses it day to day. A family member (typically an adult child) signs up and pays.

What it does

On any day with an active extreme-heat advisory, sends a single SMS check-in. No reply escalates to a neighbor.

Full concept: wiki/concept.md

How it works

1
Extreme-heat advisory is issuedThat's the trigger — nothing happens on an ordinary day.
↓
2
Farmer gets one SMS check-inSent to a basic phone, no app required.
↓
3
Farmer replies — or doesn'tA reply within the window ends it there.
↓
4
No reply → the pre-designated neighbor is contactedThey check on the farmer in person.

What this doesn't do yet

Deliberately out of scope

  • Farmers with no reachable neighbor. Enrollment requires naming one. Both named cases in our source — the Jinju greenhouse, the Jeju rice paddy — were people found only after collapsing, with no one nearby. A neighbor-based service does not yet reach that exact situation. (Korea heat deaths, elderly)
  • Deaths at home or near roads. Of the 238 recorded deaths, 15% happened at home and roughly 14% near roads — this service is scoped to farm work, not those settings. (Korea heat deaths, elderly)
