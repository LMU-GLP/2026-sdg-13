# LA's New Chief Heat Officer Expands Cooling Centers Based on Equity Maps

- **Source:** Esri (newsroom blog)
- **URL:** https://www.esri.com/about/newsroom/blog/los-angeles-chief-heat-officer-expands-cooling-centers
- **Read on:** 2026-07-31

## Key figures

- Marta Segura is Los Angeles's **first chief heat officer**, appointed **June 2022**; she also leads the city's Climate Emergency Mobilization Office.
- The city expanded augmented cooling centers **from 6 to 22 locations** in underserved neighborhoods.
- The **Cool Spots LA** web app maps **more than 200 cooling options** — libraries, recreation centers, pools, hydration stations, and shade structures — built with GIS equity mapping.
- During the **August 2022 heat dome**, emergency departments treated **an additional 1,500 patients daily**.
- LA now experiences **six times more heatwaves** than historically.

## Direct quotes

> "Some neighborhoods without trees, shade, and open space suffer four times the number of hospitalizations and premature deaths than areas with a greener, more climate-adapted infrastructure."

> "We had the highest cooling center attendance we have ever had, in part because of our app."

> "This is how constituents expect city leaders to lead—we must cocreate and codesign services and projects—seamlessly."

## Summary

Los Angeles already runs a program aimed squarely at this challenge: a chief heat officer role, backed by GIS equity mapping, that grew the city's augmented cooling centers from 6 to 22 and built a public app mapping over 200 cooling options. It's evidence that neighborhood-level, equity-targeted heat response is an active model, not a hypothetical — and a benchmark any new proposal for LA should be measured against.
